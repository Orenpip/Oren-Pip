Solar Car Aerobody

Leading the Aerobody and Composites Subteam at the University of Virginia's Solar Car Team — designing and fabricating lightweight, aerodynamically optimized carbon fiber structures for solar-powered racing vehicles.

Active Build Aerodynamics Leadership

The Solar Car Team at UVA is an elite, student-led experiential learning initiative within the School of Engineering. The project focuses on designing, manufacturing, and racing highly optimized, street-legal vehicles powered entirely by sunlight. By transforming theoretical photovoltaic concepts into tangible engineering solutions, the team builds ultra-lightweight vehicles engineered to compete at endurance events like the national American Solar Challenge.

Aerobody & Composites Subteam Lead

As the Aerobody and Composites Subteam Lead, I direct the mechanical division responsible for the vehicle's structural shell and aerodynamic efficiency. My technical contributions and leadership responsibilities include:

Aerodynamic Optimization
Running complex Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations to relentlessly lower the vehicle's drag coefficient and maximize overall energy efficiency.
Advanced 3D Surface Modeling
Overseeing the complete CAD development of the outer aeroshell to guarantee a flawless, high-tolerance fit over the internal chassis components.
Composite Manufacturing
Managing the hands-on shop operations for layout, vacuum bagging, and curing lightweight carbon fiber structures to minimize vehicle weight while maintaining rigorous structural safety standards.
Team Leadership
Directing subteam operations, coordinating with other engineering divisions (chassis, electrical, drivetrain), and ensuring all components integrate seamlessly for competition-ready performance.
