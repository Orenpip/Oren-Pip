UVA Rocketry Team · Air-Brakes Subteam
International Rocket Engineering Competition

Air brakes are drag-inducing fins that deploy during ascent to bleed off velocity and hit a precise target altitude. This is a critical system for competition success, as hitting the target altitude (typically 10,000 feet at IREC) is a major scoring component.

The air-brake system requires real-time actuation based on onboard sensor data, balancing structural loads at high dynamic pressure while maintaining responsiveness and precision. Unlike traditional parachute recovery systems, air brakes allow the rocket to maintain altitude control throughout flight.

My responsibilities on this subteam include:

  • Actuator mechanism design — mechanical systems to deploy and retract the brake panels in flight
  • Bracket and deployment geometry — ensuring the fins deploy cleanly with minimal aerodynamic interference
  • Structural analysis — calculating loads during deployment and validating safety margins
  • Flight computer integration — coordinating with the avionics team to receive and execute deployment commands
